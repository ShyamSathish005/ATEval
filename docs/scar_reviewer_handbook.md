# SCAR reviewer handbook

## Purpose and blinding

This study evaluates academic revision usefulness and technical meaning
preservation. Do not guess whether text was produced by a model. Candidate
labels are randomized pseudonyms; model identities, benchmark metrics, and
detector scores are not shown.

## Editing necessity

Choose `NO_EDIT_REQUIRED` when the passage is acceptable for its academic
purpose and no concrete improvement is needed. Choose a grammar, clarity,
redundancy, or structural label only when a specific change would improve the
passage without changing its research claim. Choose `TECHNICAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED`
when domain expertise is needed. Choose `INSUFFICIENT_CONTEXT` when the
available passage cannot support a responsible judgment.

## Candidate assessment

- **IMPROVED**: clearer or more correct without a material loss or addition.
- **EQUIVALENT**: acceptable and meaning-preserving, but not materially better.
- **WORSE**: less clear, less precise, or unnecessarily rewritten.
- **UNACCEPTABLE**: contains a material scientific, numerical, citation, or
  grammatical failure.
- **CANNOT_ASSESS**: outside your expertise or missing necessary context.

Use the no-edit option when the original is already adequate. Distinguish
grammar from stylistic preference: a preference is not an error unless it
impairs clarity, correctness, or disciplinary appropriateness.

## Preservation checklist

Check scientific meaning, numbers and units, technical terms, causal
relationships, qualifications such as “may” or “only,” limitations, and
citations separately. A fluent paraphrase can still alter a claim. Use
`CANNOT_ASSESS` rather than guessing about unfamiliar technical content.

## Confidence and comments

Confidence is ordinal: 1 very uncertain, 2 somewhat uncertain, 3 moderately
confident, 4 confident, 5 very confident. Explain material disagreements and
identify the changed phrase. Reviewers may abstain at any time.
